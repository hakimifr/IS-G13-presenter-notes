# Q and A bank | Anticipated questions with model answers

Rubric item: Q and A ("conducted the session very well"). Any presenter can answer, but the best match is shown in brackets. Keep each answer to about 30 to 45 seconds. If you do not know, say so honestly and offer to check, and do not guess a ruling. Our lecturer is the final authority.

---

**1. If a person is forced into prostitution, are they sinful? [Presenter 3]**
Surah An-Nur: Verse 33 says that if someone compels them, then Allah is, to them, after their compulsion, Forgiving and Merciful. The responsibility is on the one who compels. This is why we separate the victim from the exploiter. If a person was deceived, threatened or held in debt bondage, we treat them as a victim and not as a criminal.

**2. Isn't legalising prostitution better, so that workers are safe? [Presenter 4]**
It is a real debate and we do not claim it is closed. The study by Cho, Dreher and Neumayer across up to 150 countries found that, on average, countries with legal prostitution reported larger trafficking inflows. Other researchers argue decriminalisation can improve workers' safety. Our point is that the Islamic position does not depend on that debate. The act is forbidden, the exploiter is accountable, the victim is protected, and the community has a duty to prevent and to support. We would also say that punishing victims is not the Islamic way.

**3. What about poverty? Isn't it a necessity (darurah)? [Presenter 3 or 5]**
Poverty is real, and Islam takes it seriously with zakat, the duty to feed the poor and the call in Surah An-Nur: Verse 32 to help people marry. The community is blamed for leaving a person without options. But scholars do not treat hardship as a licence for zina. They do treat compulsion, which means force or threat, as removing blame, as Surah An-Nur: Verse 33 shows. If the question goes deeper into fiqh, we should say that we are students and refer to the scholars.

**4. Does Malaysian law punish the buyer? [Presenter 2]**
The Penal Code mainly targets exploiters, intermediaries, people living on the earnings, advertisers and soliciting (sections 372, 372A, 372B). For Muslims, the Syariah law has the offence of sexual intercourse out of wedlock for both man and woman (section 23) and acts preparatory to it (section 24), which can cover buyers. We have not found a general federal offence for simply buying sex between adults, so please correct us if you know of one.

**5. Why does the Quran mention slave girls in Surah An-Nur: Verse 33? Does that mean Islam allows slavery? [Presenter 3]**
The verse was revealed to stop a practice where masters forced slave girls to earn money. It is a protection for the weak and it uses the situation that existed at that time. Modern slavery and trafficking are forbidden, and the hadith in Bukhari 2227 says Allah will oppose the one who sells a free person and eats the price. The principle in the verse, do not compel people into prostitution for profit, applies fully today.

**6. Is it fair that the woman is blamed more than the man? [Presenter 2 or 4]**
The Quran addresses both. Surah An-Nur: Verse 2 names the woman and the man, and Surah An-Nur: Verses 30 and 31 give instructions to men and women. In the Federal Territories Act, section 21(1) names "any woman who prostitutes herself", and critics raise that. But section 23 covers both a man and a woman for sexual intercourse out of wedlock, and the muncikari offence (section 22) covers any person. We think the Islamic principle is that both sides are accountable.

**7. Shouldn't there be harm reduction, like free HIV tests, for sex workers? [Presenter 4]**
Protecting life is one of the aims of the Shariah, and seeking treatment is encouraged in Islam. The studies show that stigma keeps people away from testing. So we think testing, treatment and counselling can be offered with respect while still saying clearly that the act is forbidden. Offering medical care to a person is not approval of the sin.

**8. How can I help a friend who is struggling? [Presenter 5]**
Speak privately and listen first. Do not shame them or tell others. Remind them gently of Allah's mercy, like the Prophet (SAW) did with the young man. Help them reach a trusted person, a counsellor or an imam. For safety, Talian Kasih 15999 and the university counselling service can help. If there is danger or force, report to the police.

**9. Is a "sugar baby" or "sugar daddy" arrangement the same as prostitution? [Presenter 1]**
If sex is exchanged for money or benefits, then yes, it falls under the same ruling, and the Malaysian definition covers payment "in kind". Even if sex is not part of the arrangement, the secret meetings and the dependence on someone's money are the early steps on the staircase, and Islam tells us to stay away from them.

**10. Some people say they chose it and they are happy. How do you respond? [Presenter 4]**
We do not deny that people have different experiences, and we do not judge anyone's heart. The studies we quoted show that many people want to leave, and the ruling of Islam does not depend on feelings. We also say that the buyer and the pimp share responsibility, so the case is not only about one person's choice.

**11. If the prostitute in the hadith was forgiven for giving water to a dog, does it mean the sin is small? [Presenter 1 or 3]**
No. The hadith shows the vastness of Allah's mercy and the value of a sincere good deed, and it does not make the act lawful. Allah forgives whom He wills, and Al-Furqan 68 to 70 tells us that the way to forgiveness is sincere repentance, belief and righteous deeds.

**12. Are hudud punishments for zina carried out in Malaysia? [Presenter 2 or 3]**
No. Syariah criminal offences in Malaysia are tried as ta'zir offences under state enactments, within the federal limit of three years in prison, RM5,000 and six strokes of the cane. The Quran also sets a very high standard of proof, four witnesses in Surah An-Nur: Verse 4, and warns against accusing people, so the focus is on prevention and not on spying.

**13. What about men or LGBTQ people in prostitution? [Presenter 1]**
The principles are the same for everyone. Selling sex is forbidden, forcing and exploiting is forbidden, and victims should be protected. Our topic here is prostitution, and Syariah law has other provisions on other matters that we have not covered.

**14. Why did you choose to talk about solutions and not only punishments? [Presenter 5]**
Because the Prophet (SAW) did. He closed the doors, he used reason, he made du'a for the young man, and the Quran keeps the door of repentance open. A talk that only warns would leave out half of the Islamic response.

**15. What can a student do today? [Presenter 5]**
Three things. Guard the first step, which means private chats and secret offers. Never be the buyer or the middleman and speak up among friends. Be a safe person for someone who is struggling, and know where to get help.
