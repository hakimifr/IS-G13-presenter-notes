## Slide 4 | The woman and the dog (1:15 to 2:30)

Let me start with a story from Sahih al-Bukhari, number 3467. Abu Hurairah reported that the Prophet (SAW) said: a dog was circling a well and was about to die of thirst. A woman from Bani Israel, a prostitute, saw it. She took off her leather sock, lowered it into the well and gave the dog water. Allah forgave her because of that.

[Pause for two seconds.]

The Prophet (SAW) did not hide what her life was. He named it. And still Allah looked at one sincere act of mercy and forgave her.

This story is no licence. The act is forbidden, and Presenter 3 will show the evidence. The story gives us the attitude for today. Islam is firm about the sin and the exploitation around it, and it never closes the door of mercy on a human being. Our whole talk follows three words from this attitude, Protect, Prevent and Restore, and you will see them again in Part 5. Two ground rules for the room. This is a serious topic, so no jokes about people. And many people in prostitution were deceived, in debt or forced, so we speak about them with respect.

## Slide 5 | Warm-up: myth or fact? (2:30 to 3:20)

Let us warm up. I will read four statements. Raise your hand if you think the statement is true. [Read slowly and count hands. Do not give the answers yet.]

One. Selling sex is a form of zina in Islam.
Two. Only the person selling is sinful, the buyer is not.
Three. Everyone in prostitution chose it freely.
Four. A person who leaves prostitution can be forgiven.

We come back to these at the end, when you will know the evidence. [If you are short of time, give the answers now: one is true, two is false, three is false in many cases, four is true.]

## Slide 6 | What is prostitution? (3:20 to 4:20)

Three angles.

In language, the Arabic word is bigha', from a root that means seeking and also transgressing. The woman is called a baghiy. Allah uses the word in Surah An-Nur: Verse 33. In Malay we say pelacuran.

In fiqh, prostitution is zina done for a price. Zina is sexual relations outside a valid marriage. Payment does not change that ruling, and it adds a second problem because the money itself is forbidden (mahr al-baghiy). Presenter 3 will show the hadith.

In Malaysian law, section 372(3) of the Penal Code says prostitution is "the act of a person offering that person's body for sexual gratification for hire whether in money or in kind". Notice "in kind". Payment can be gifts, allowances, a phone or a holiday, not only cash.

## Slide 7 | Zina, prostitution and trafficking (4:20 to 5:05)

These three overlap but they are different. Zina is the widest. Prostitution is zina with payment. Trafficking adds force, fraud or exploitation, and there the person is the victim of a crime.

The ruling on the act is the same. The blame is not. Someone who is forced is in a very different position from someone who profits, and the one who sells a free person is condemned in Bukhari 2227. In Surah An-Nur: Verse 33, Allah promises forgiveness to those who were compelled.

## Slide 8 | The scale of the problem (5:05 to 6:00)

Now some numbers, with a warning first. This is a hidden activity, so every number is an estimate.

Globally, the ILO, Walk Free and IOM estimated in 2022 that 50 million people lived in modern slavery in 2021. Of all people in forced labour, 23 percent were in forced commercial sexual exploitation, and almost four in five of them were women or girls.

In Malaysia, the Ministry of Health estimated about 37,000 women are sex workers. A study in Greater Kuala Lumpur found HIV prevalence of 11.7 percent among them, against 0.4 percent among adults in general. Presenter 4 will return to this. And the 2024 US State Department report says 165 trafficking victims were identified in Malaysia, 29 of them in sex trafficking.

## Backup slides for this part (hidden, not part of the live talk)

Use these if a question needs them, or if the lecturer gives you more time.

### Backup slide 44 | Our approach in three words

Protect the vulnerable from exploitation. Prevent people from taking the first step. Restore people who want to leave. Ground rules for the room: no jokes about people, and speak with respect because many in prostitution were deceived, in debt or forced.

### Backup slide 45 | Key terms

Zina, unlawful sexual relations. Bigha' and baghiy, the act and the woman. Mahr al-baghiy, the fee earned from prostitution. Fahisha, an abomination, the word the Quran uses for zina. Khalwat, being alone with someone of the opposite sex who is not your spouse or mahram. Muncikari, a procurer. Our Syariah law (Act 559, section 2) defines it as a person who acts as a procurer between a female and a male for any purpose contrary to Islamic law. Tawbah, sincere repentance. Haya', shame before Allah and modesty.

### Backup slide 46 | Forms today

Prostitution is not only a street corner. It happens on the street, in massage or entertainment premises, through escort agencies, and increasingly online through social media, Telegram groups and webcams. There are also sugar arrangements, where allowances and gifts are exchanged for intimacy. The ruling is the same, and the Malaysian definition covers "in kind" payment. At the darkest end is trafficking and forced prostitution.

### Backup slide 47 | Why this matters to us

We are young, online most of the day, many of us live away from family, and some feel money pressure. The first step rarely looks like prostitution. It looks like a message from a stranger, an easy offer or a friend's invitation. Islam teaches us to look at the first step, which is the idea Presenter 2 starts with.
