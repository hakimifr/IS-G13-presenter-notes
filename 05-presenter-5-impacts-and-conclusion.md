## Slide 29 | Part 5 divider (0:00 to 0:10)

Thank you, Presenter 4. We have seen what the Quran, the Sunnah and the research say. Now the question every Muslim should ask. What does this do to us, and what do we do about it?

## Slide 30 | Impact 1: on the individual (0:10 to 0:55)

First, the individual, both the one who sells and the one who buys.

The soul. The Prophet (SAW) said in Bukhari 2475 that the adulterer is not a believer at the time he does it. Repeated sin dulls the heart and haya' slowly disappears.

The mind and self-worth. 68 percent in Presenter 4's study met the criteria for PTSD. A person treated as a product starts to see themselves as one, and the buyer's heart is trained to see people as things.

Honesty and trust. A life of secrecy builds the habit of lying to parents, friends and a future spouse.

## Slide 31 | Impact 2: on the family and society (0:55 to 1:45)

Second, the family and society. Marriage in Islam rests on tranquillity, affection and mercy (Surah Ar-Rum: Verse 21). Paying for sex breaks trust between spouses and can bring disease home. Children suffer when parents break apart or a father refuses responsibility.

A community that treats people as a commodity loses its sense of dignity, and parents become afraid for their children. Online content normalises the idea, and Allah warns those who love that immorality spreads (Surah An-Nur: Verse 19). Crime follows the money, because pimps, loan sharks and traffickers profit from it. The Prophet (SAW) gave the parable of people on a ship, where everyone sinks if nobody stops the one who damages it. A community is responsible for its own ship.

## Slide 32 | Impact 3: on the ummah and the nation (1:45 to 2:25)

Third, the ummah and the nation. Earnings from it are haram, and a community living on haram income loses barakah, while the money feeds organised crime and trafficking networks. The health bill of more HIV and other infections falls on the whole country. And when a Muslim-majority country is named in trafficking reports, as the US report does for Malaysia, it hurts the dignity of the ummah. Allah honoured every child of Adam (Surah Al-Isra: Verse 70), so the generation that will lead must protect the vulnerable.

## Slide 33 | Protect (2:25 to 3:05)

What can we do? First, Protect. Know the signs of trafficking. A person who cannot leave. A passport or identity card held by someone else. A debt that keeps growing. A job that is not what was promised. In the Penal Code, section 372(2), holding a person's passport or identity card, or threatening them over debt, is presumed to be wrongful restraint.

If you see it or suspect it, call 999 in an emergency, or call Talian Kasih 15999, the helpline of the Ministry of Women, Family and Community Development. You do not need to be sure, the authorities investigate.

## Slide 34 | Prevent (3:05 to 3:55)

Second, Prevent. This is where students have the most power.

Personally, guard the gaze and the private chat, avoid being alone with someone of the opposite sex, and stay busy with fasting, exercise and friends who remind you of Allah. Watch the first step. The red flags online are a secret offer, a payment far too big for what is asked, a "job" that wants your passport or IC first, and a stranger who builds trust slowly and then asks for more. If you see them, talk to someone before you accept.

As a community, make halal ways easy by supporting those ready to marry, and let a friend say "I am struggling" without being laughed at. On the demand side, there is no market without buyers. Men carry a special duty to refuse and to speak up among friends. Never be the buyer, the middleman or the silent bystander.

## Slide 35 | Restore (3:55 to 4:45)

Third, Restore, the part we often forget. Allah says in Surah Al-Furqan: Verse 70 that He replaces the evil deeds of the one who repents with good. The woman who gave the dog water shows that one sincere good act matters to Allah. The Prophet (SAW) prayed for the young man and did not shame him.

So when a person leaves this life, our job is to avoid reminding them of their past, protect their privacy, help them find lawful work and welcome them back to the community and the mosque. If a friend is struggling, speak privately, listen first and help them reach a counsellor, an imam or a trusted adult. If you need help yourself, university counselling and Talian Kasih 15999 are there.

## Slide 36 | Conclusion: three messages (4:45 to 5:25)

Three messages for all of us.

One. Guard the first step. Allah said do not even approach zina, so close the early doors, the gaze, the private chat and the secret offer.

Two. Condemn the exploitation and protect the weak. Do not be a buyer, a middleman or a silent bystander. The one who forces is accountable, and the one forced is shown mercy (Surah An-Nur: Verse 33).

Three. Keep the door of tawbah open. Do not push a person back with shame. Allah replaces evil deeds with good for the one who repents (Surah Al-Furqan: Verse 70).

## Slide 37 | Back to the poll, and a du'a (5:25 to 6:00)

Back to our four statements. Selling sex is zina with a price, true. Only the seller is sinful, false, because the buyer and the middleman are accountable. Everyone chose it freely, false in many cases because of deception, debt and force. A person who leaves can be forgiven, true, and that is Allah's promise in Surah Al-Furqan: Verse 70.

We finish with the Prophet's du'a for the young man, which is a du'a for all of us. *Allahummaghfir dhanbah, wa tahhir qalbah, wa hassin farjah.* O Allah, forgive our sins, purify our hearts and guard our chastity. Ameen.

## Backup slides for this part (hidden, not part of the live talk)

### Backup slide 60 | Our reality: the digital age

The first step is now on our phone. Sugar arrangements on social media. Private groups that sell content. "Easy job" adverts from a fake company. Strangers who build trust slowly and then ask for more. Because money is attached to everything, it can look like a normal side income. Watch the first step. If the offer is secret, the payment is too big for what is asked, or someone asks you to hide it from your parents, that is the staircase. Walk down, not up. The slide shows four chat messages and four red flags: a secret, a big payment, your documents, slow trust.

### Backup slide 61 | Case cards: what would you do?

Pick one or two cards. Ask the audience "What is it? What does Islam say? What can we do?" and then give the model answer.

Card A. Mei Ling and Zul are offered a hotel job abroad. On arrival their passports are taken and they are told they owe a debt. This is trafficking, covered by section 372 and the Anti-Trafficking Act, and Bukhari 2227 is against the one who sells a free person. A friend can contact the embassy, the police and the family, and report.

Card B. After the exam, friends say "Let us go to that place, everyone does it once." It is an invitation, the first step on the staircase. Say no clearly, leave, and tell a trusted friend. Remember the Prophet's question, "Would you like it for your sister?" And remember the buyer is part of the market.

Card C. A woman returns to the village after leaving sex work and the neighbours whisper. Surah Al-Furqan: Verse 70 says the door of tawbah is open. We should not spread her past, because Allah warned about spreading immorality in Surah An-Nur: Verse 19. We help her find work and be part of the community again.
