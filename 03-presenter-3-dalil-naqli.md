## Slide 15 | Part 3 divider (0:00 to 0:10)

Thank you, Presenter 2. Now the evidence that matters most for us as Muslims, the dalil naqli. The Quran first, then the hadith.

## Slide 16 | Surah Al-Isra: Verse 32 (0:10 to 1:00)

Allah says: *wa la taqrabu az-zina, innahu kana fahishah wa sa'a sabila*. "And do not approach unlawful sexual intercourse. Indeed, it is ever an immorality and is evil as a way."

Allah did not say do not commit zina. He said do not approach it. Scholars such as Ibn Kathir explain that this covers everything that leads to it, the looks, the private meetings and the conversations. The verse gives two reasons. It is a fahisha, an abomination in itself, and it is "evil as a way" because it brings more harm to the person, the family and society. This is why Islam fights the early steps on Presenter 2's staircase.

## Slide 17 | Surah An-Nur: Verse 33 (1:00 to 2:20)

This verse speaks about prostitution by name. Allah says: *wa la tukrihu fatayatikum 'ala al-bigha' in aradna tahassunan litabtaghu 'arada al-hayati ad-dunya wa man yukrihhunna fa inna Allaha min ba'di ikrahihinna ghafurun rahim.* "And do not compel your slave girls to prostitution, if they desire chastity, to seek the temporary interests of worldly life. And if someone should compel them, then indeed, Allah is, to them, after their compulsion, Forgiving and Merciful."

Three things to notice. Al-bigha' is the exact word for prostitution. The motive Allah names is "the temporary interests of worldly life", which is money, so earning from another person's body is condemned. And the forgiveness and mercy go to the women who were compelled. The sin of compulsion is on the one who forced them.

Jabir reported in Sahih Muslim that the verse came down about a man in Madinah who forced his slave girls to earn money this way, and they complained to the Prophet (SAW). The verse protects the weak party and condemns the exploiter. Today that means trafficking, forced prostitution and debt bondage. The verse before it, Surah An-Nur: Verse 32, tells the community to help the unmarried marry, even the poor, because Allah will enrich them. For those who cannot marry yet, Surah An-Nur: Verse 33 tells them to abstain. Islam does not only say no, it tells the community to make the lawful way easy.

## Slide 18 | Surah An-Nur: Verses 2, 4 and 19 (2:20 to 3:20)

Surah An-Nur: Verse 2 gives the punishment for zina, caning for each of them, the man and the woman, witnessed by a group of believers. For context, the Sunnah gives a different punishment for a married person, and in Malaysia these hudud punishments are not carried out. The point is that zina is a harm to society and not only a private sin.

Surah An-Nur: Verse 4 sets a very high bar. Whoever accuses chaste people of zina and does not bring four witnesses is caned eighty times and his testimony is rejected. Islam does not want spying and rumours. The aim is to guard honour. And Surah An-Nur: Verse 19 warns that those who love to see immorality spread among the believers will have a painful punishment. Spreading rumours or immoral content online is part of the problem.

## Slide 19 | Surah Al-Furqan: Verses 68 to 70 (3:20 to 4:00)

Allah describes the servants of the Most Merciful, and among them "those who do not commit unlawful sexual intercourse". Whoever does these things meets a penalty, "except for those who repent, believe and do righteous work. For them Allah will replace their evil deeds with good."

The door is open. Allah does more than forgive, He replaces bad deeds with good ones. That is the Restore part of our approach, and we return to it in Part 5.

## Slide 20 | Hadith 1: the price of a prostitute (4:00 to 4:40)

Abu Mas'ud al-Ansari said, in Sahih al-Bukhari 2237, that the Messenger of Allah (SAW) forbade the price of a dog, the mahr al-baghiy, and the fee of a soothsayer. Mahr al-baghiy is what a prostitute earns.

The scholars treat the earnings as haram. Even if someone says "I only do this to earn", the Prophet (SAW) already closed that argument. The income sits beside the soothsayer's fee and the price of a dog, things that cheat or corrupt.

## Slide 21 | Hadith 2: the young man who asked permission (4:40 to 6:10)

This is one of my favourite hadith. It is in Musnad Ahmad 22211, narrated by Abu Umamah, and graded sahih. A young man came to the Prophet (SAW) and said, "O Messenger of Allah, give me permission to commit zina." The people around him were shocked and told him to be quiet. The Prophet (SAW) said, "Come close." He asked, "Would you like it for your mother?" The man said no, by Allah. The Prophet said, "Neither do people like it for their mothers." Then he asked about his daughter, his sister, his paternal aunt and his maternal aunt, and each time the answer was no. Then the Prophet (SAW) placed his hand on him and prayed, "O Allah, forgive his sin, purify his heart and guard his chastity." The narrator says the young man never turned to it again.

Look at the method. No shouting and no shaming. The Prophet (SAW) used the young man's own heart and sense of fairness, which Presenter 4 calls dalil aqli, and then he made du'a for him.

## Slide 22 | Summary of the naqli evidence (6:10 to 7:00)

From the Quran and the Sunnah we get seven principles. The act is forbidden, and so is approaching it. The earnings are haram. Forcing and selling people is forbidden, and in Bukhari 2227 Allah says He will oppose the one who sells a free person and eats the price. The compelled are forgiven. The community closes the early doors, with the guarded gaze (Surah An-Nur: Verses 30 and 31) and the warning against being alone together (Tirmidhi 2165), and opens the lawful way. Punishment needs very strong proof. And the door of tawbah is always open.

## Backup slides for this part (hidden, not part of the live talk)

### Backup slide 51 | Surah An-Nur: Verses 21, 30 and 31

Surah An-Nur: Verse 21: "O you who have believed, do not follow the footsteps of Shaytan. And whoever follows the footsteps of Shaytan, indeed, he enjoins immorality and wrongdoing." Surah An-Nur: Verse 30: "Tell the believing men to reduce some of their vision and guard their private parts. That is purer for them." Surah An-Nur: Verse 31 gives the same instruction to believing women, along with covering the adornment and drawing the headcover over the chest. The guard of the gaze comes first and the guard of the private parts after it, and both men and women are addressed, so the responsibility is shared.

### Backup slide 52 | Surah Al-Mu'minun: Verses 5 to 7 and Surah An-Nur: Verse 32

"And they who guard their private parts, except from their wives or those their right hands possess, for indeed, they will not be blamed. But whoever seeks beyond that, then those are the transgressors." So there is a lawful channel, which is marriage. In Surah An-Nur: Verse 32, Allah tells the community, "And marry the unmarried among you... if they should be poor, Allah will enrich them from His bounty", and in Surah An-Nur: Verse 33, "let them abstain" for those who cannot marry yet. The community must make marriage easy and support the person who is struggling. That is the Prevent part of our approach.

### Backup slide 53 | Hadith 3 and 4: faith, and selling a free person

Sahih al-Bukhari 2475 (Abu Hurairah): the Prophet (SAW) said, "The adulterer does not commit adultery while he is a believer." Scholars explain that this does not mean he leaves Islam. The fullness and light of faith leave him at that moment, which shows how serious it is, and that faith in the heart should stop the hand.

Sahih al-Bukhari 2227: Allah says He will oppose three people on the Day of Resurrection, and one of them is the person who sells a free person and eats the price. This is the hadith against human trafficking, and it is a hadith qudsi, the words of Allah.

### Backup slide 54 | Hadith 5 to 7: closing the doors

Tirmidhi 2165, graded sahih: "A man is not alone with a woman except that the third of them is Shaytan." Sahih al-Bukhari 5233: "No man should be alone with a woman except with a mahram." Sahih al-Bukhari 5066: "O young people, whoever among you can marry, let him marry, because it lowers the gaze and guards the private parts. And whoever cannot, let him fast, for fasting is a shield for him." For us as students this means care with private chats and meetings, marrying when ready, and healthy ways of handling desire like fasting and keeping busy.
