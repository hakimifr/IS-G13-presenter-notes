## Slide 22 | Part 3 divider (0:00 to 0:15)

Thank you, Presenter 2. Now the evidence that matters most for us as Muslims, the dalil naqli. We will go through the Quran first, then the hadith.

## Slide 23 | Surah Al-Isra: Verse 32 (0:15 to 1:15)

Allah says: *wa la taqrabu az-zina, innahu kana fahishah wa sa'a sabila*. "And do not approach unlawful sexual intercourse. Indeed, it is ever an immorality and is evil as a way."

Look at the first words. Allah did not say do not commit zina. He said do not approach it. Scholars such as Ibn Kathir explain that this includes everything that leads to it, the looks, the private meetings and the conversations. The verse also gives two reasons. It is a fahisha, an abomination in itself, and it is "evil as a way" because it leads to more harm for the person, the family and society. This one verse already tells us why Islam fights the early steps that Presenter 2 showed on the staircase.

## Slide 24 | Surah An-Nur: Verse 33 (1:15 to 2:30)

This verse speaks about prostitution by name. Allah says: *wa la tukrihu fatayatikum 'ala al-bigha' in aradna tahassunan litabtaghu 'arada al-hayati ad-dunya wa man yukrihhunna fa inna Allaha min ba'di ikrahihinna ghafurun rahim.* "And do not compel your slave girls to prostitution, if they desire chastity, to seek the temporary interests of worldly life. And if someone should compel them, then indeed, Allah is, to them, after their compulsion, Forgiving and Merciful."

Three things to notice. First, the word al-bigha' is the exact word for prostitution. Second, the motive Allah names is "to seek the temporary interests of worldly life", which is money. So Allah forbids earning from another person's body, and forbids forcing. Third, the last sentence. The promise of forgiveness and mercy goes to the women who were compelled. The sin of the compulsion is on the one who forced them.

Jabir ibn Abdullah reported, in Sahih Muslim, that this verse came down about a man in Madinah who forced his slave girls to earn money this way, and they complained to the Prophet (SAW). So the verse protects the weak party and condemns the exploiter. For us today, that means trafficking, forced prostitution and debt bondage are exactly what this verse is against.

## Slide 25 | Surah An-Nur: Verses 2, 4 and 19 (2:30 to 3:45)

Verse 2 gives the punishment for zina, the lash for each of them, the man and the woman, and it says the punishment should be witnessed by a group of believers. A note for context. The Sunnah gives a different punishment for a married person, and in Malaysia these hudud punishments are not carried out. What we have here is the principle that zina is a crime against society and not only a private sin.

But look at verse 4. Allah says that whoever accuses chaste people of zina and does not bring four witnesses should be lashed eighty times and his testimony rejected. This sets a very high bar. Islam does not want people spying on each other or spreading accusations. The aim is to guard honour. And verse 19 warns that those who love to see immorality spread among the believers will have a painful punishment in this world and the Hereafter. So spreading rumours, and spreading pornographic or immoral content online, are also part of the problem.

## Slide 26 | Surah An-Nur: Verses 21 and 30 to 31 (3:45 to 4:45)

Verse 21: "O you who have believed, do not follow the footsteps of Shaytan. And whoever follows the footsteps of Shaytan, indeed, he enjoins immorality and wrongdoing."

And verses 30 and 31 give the practical guard. "Tell the believing men to reduce some of their vision and guard their private parts. That is purer for them." And the same instruction for believing women, along with covering the adornment and the headcover over the chest.

Notice that the guard of the gaze comes first and the guard of the private parts comes after. And both men and women are addressed, so the responsibility is shared.

## Slide 27 | Surah Al-Mu'minun: Verses 5 to 7 and Surah An-Nur: Verse 32 (4:45 to 5:45)

Here Allah describes the believers who succeed. "And they who guard their private parts, except from their wives or those their right hands possess, for indeed, they will not be blamed. But whoever seeks beyond that, then those are the transgressors."

So there is a lawful channel, which is marriage. And in Surah An-Nur 32 Allah tells the community, "And marry the unmarried among you... if they should be poor, Allah will enrich them from His bounty". And in verse 33 for those who cannot marry yet, "let them abstain".

This is important. Islam does not only say no. It tells the community to make marriage easy, and to support the person who is struggling. That is the Prevent part of our approach.

## Slide 28 | Surah Al-Furqan: Verses 68 to 70 (5:45 to 6:30)

Allah describes the servants of the Most Merciful, and among them "those who do not commit unlawful sexual intercourse". Then He says whoever does these things will meet a penalty, "except for those who repent, believe and do righteous work. For them Allah will replace their evil deeds with good."

So the door is open. Allah does not only forgive. He replaces bad deeds with good deeds. That is the Restore part of our approach, and we will return to it in Part 5.

## Slide 29 | Hadith 1: the price of a prostitute (6:30 to 7:15)

Abu Mas'ud al-Ansari said, in Sahih al-Bukhari 2237, that the Messenger of Allah (SAW) forbade the price of a dog, the mahr al-baghiy, and the fee of a soothsayer. Mahr al-baghiy is what a prostitute earns.

The scholars explain that this fee is haram, and that the people who take it must not benefit from it. So even if someone says "I only do this to earn", the Prophet (SAW) already closed that argument. The income is unclean. It is placed beside the soothsayer's fee and the price of a dog, things that cheat or corrupt.

## Slide 30 | Hadith 2 and 3: faith and selling a free person (7:15 to 8:00)

In Sahih al-Bukhari 2475, Abu Hurairah reported that the Prophet (SAW) said, "The adulterer does not commit adultery while he is a believer." Scholars explain that this does not mean the person leaves Islam. It means the fullness of faith and the light of faith leave him at that moment. It shows how serious it is, and that faith in the heart should stop the hand.

And in Sahih al-Bukhari 2227, Allah says three people He will oppose on the Day of Judgment. One of them is the person who sells a free person and eats the price. This is the hadith against human trafficking, and it is a hadith qudsi, the words of Allah.

## Slide 31 | Hadith 4: the young man who asked permission (8:00 to 9:00)

This is one of my favourite hadith. It is in Musnad Ahmad 22211, narrated by Abu Umamah, and graded sahih. A young man came to the Prophet (SAW) and said, "O Messenger of Allah, give me permission to commit zina." The people around him were shocked and told him to be quiet. The Prophet (SAW) said, "Come close." He asked, "Would you like it for your mother?" He said no, by Allah. The Prophet said, "Neither do people like it for their mothers." Then he asked the same about his daughter, his sister, his paternal aunt and his maternal aunt, and each time the answer was no. Then the Prophet (SAW) placed his hand on him and prayed, "O Allah, forgive his sin, purify his heart and guard his chastity." And the narrator says the young man never turned to anything of it again.

Look at the method. No shouting, no shaming. He used the young man's own heart and his sense of fairness, which is what Presenter 4 will call dalil aqli. And then he made du'a for him.

## Slide 32 | Hadith 5 to 7: closing the doors (9:00 to 9:45)

Three hadith about prevention. In Tirmidhi 2165, graded sahih, "A man is not alone with a woman except that the third of them is Shaytan." In Sahih al-Bukhari 5233, "No man should be alone with a woman except with a mahram." And in Sahih al-Bukhari 5066, the Prophet (SAW) says, "O young people, whoever among you can marry, let him marry, because it lowers the gaze and guards the private parts. And whoever cannot, let him fast, for fasting is a shield for him."

For us as students, this means being careful with private chats and meetings, making marriage possible when we are ready, and having healthy ways of controlling desire, like fasting and keeping busy.

## Slide 33 | Summary of the naqli evidence (9:45 to 10:15)

To sum up. From the Quran and the Sunnah we get seven principles. The act is forbidden, and so is approaching it. The earnings are haram. Forcing and selling people is forbidden. The compelled are forgiven. The community must close the doors and open the lawful way. Punishments have a very high standard of proof. And the door of tawbah is always open.
