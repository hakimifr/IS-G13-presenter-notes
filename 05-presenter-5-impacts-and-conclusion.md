# Presenter 5 | Impacts, what we can do, conclusion (slides 45 to 56, about 9 to 10 minutes)

Goal of this part: connect everything to our morals and ethics today, give practical steps, and finish with three clear messages. Rubric items: Impacts ("good reflections to Muslims' moral and ethics") and Conclusion ("clear messages delivered"). Also hosts the Q and A.

---

## Slide 45 | Part 5 divider (0:00 to 0:15)

Thank you, Presenter 4. We have seen what the Quran, the Sunnah and the research say. Now the question every Muslim should ask. What does this do to us, and what do we do about it?

## Slide 46 | Impact 1: on the individual (0:15 to 1:30)

First impact. On the individual, both the person who sells and the person who buys.

For the soul, the Prophet (SAW) said in Bukhari 2475 that the adulterer is not a believer at the time he commits it. Repeated sin dulls the heart, and haya', the sense of shame before Allah, slowly disappears. A person who once felt guilty starts to feel nothing.

For the mind and self-worth, the trauma figures from Presenter 4 matter. Sixty-eight percent in that study met the criteria for post-traumatic stress disorder. A person who is treated as a product starts to see themselves as one. And for the buyer, the habit trains the heart to see people as things to be used. That is a serious moral damage to the buyer as well.

For honesty and trust, a life of secrecy builds a habit of lying, to parents, to friends and to a future spouse.

## Slide 47 | Impact 2: on the family and society (1:30 to 2:45)

Second impact. On the family and society.

Marriage in Islam is built on tranquillity, affection and mercy, from Ar-Rum 21. Infidelity and paying for sex break the trust between spouses, and can bring diseases home. Children suffer when parents break apart, or when a child is born without a father who takes responsibility.

On the wider society, a community that treats people as a commodity slowly loses its sense of dignity. Parents become afraid for their children. Online content normalises the idea. Crime follows the money, because pimps, loan sharks and traffickers profit from it. Allah warns in An-Nur 19 about those who love for immorality to spread, and the Prophet (SAW) gave the parable of the people on a ship in Sahih al-Bukhari, where the whole group sinks if nobody stops those who damage it. A community is responsible for its own ship.

## Slide 48 | Impact 3: on the ummah and the nation (2:45 to 3:45)

Third impact. On the ummah and the nation.

The money. Presenter 3 showed that earnings from it are haram, and a community that lives on haram income loses barakah. It also feeds organised crime and trafficking networks.

The health bill. A rise in HIV and other infections is a cost for the whole country.

And the image of Muslims. When a Muslim-majority country is known as a destination for trafficking, as the US report describes for Malaysia, it hurts the dignity of the ummah. Our role as the generation that will lead has to include protecting the vulnerable, because Allah has honoured every child of Adam in Al-Isra 70.

## Slide 49 | Our reality: the digital age (3:45 to 4:30)

What has changed in our generation is that the first step is on our phone. Sugar arrangements on social media. Private groups that sell content. "Easy job" adverts with a fake company. Strangers who build trust slowly and then ask for more. And because money is attached to everything, it can look like a normal side income.

The principle for us stays the same. Watch the first step. If the offer is secret, the payment is too big for what is being asked, or someone asks you to hide it from your parents, that is the staircase. Walk down, not up.

## Slide 50 | Protect (4:30 to 5:15)

Now what can we do. First, Protect.

Know the signs of trafficking. A person who cannot leave. A person whose passport or identity card is held by someone else. A person who owes a debt that keeps growing. A job that is not what was promised. In the Penal Code, section 372(2), holding a person's passport or identity card, or threatening them with debt, is presumed to be wrongful restraint.

If you see it or suspect it, you can report to the police, call 999 in an emergency, or call Talian Kasih 15999, which is the helpline of the Ministry of Women, Family and Community Development. You do not need to be sure. The authorities are the ones who investigate.

## Slide 51 | Prevent (5:15 to 6:15)

Second, Prevent. This is where we, as students, have the most power.

On a personal level. Guard the gaze and the private chat. Avoid being alone with someone of the opposite sex. Fast, exercise, keep busy, keep friends who remind you of Allah. Money pressure is real, so talk to someone before you accept an offer. Learn about the financial aid, zakat funds and part-time work options at our university.

On a community level. Make halal ways easy. That means supporting those who are ready to marry, instead of making weddings expensive. Create a culture where a friend can say "I am struggling" without being laughed at.

And on the demand side. The market exists because someone is buying. Men have a particular duty to refuse and to speak up among friends.

## Slide 52 | Restore (6:15 to 7:15)

Third, Restore. This is the part we often forget.

Allah says in Al-Furqan 70 that He will replace the evil deeds of the one who repents with good deeds. The hadith about the woman who gave the dog water tells us that one sincere act of goodness matters to Allah. And the Prophet (SAW) prayed for the young man, he did not shame him.

So when a person leaves this life, our job is not to remind them of their past. It is to protect their privacy, help them find lawful work, support them emotionally, and welcome them back to the community and the mosque. If a friend is struggling, speak privately, listen first, and help them reach a counsellor, an imam or a trusted adult.

If you ever need help yourself, our university counselling service and Talian Kasih 15999 are available.

## Slide 53 | Case cards (7:15 to 8:45)

[Hold up the cards. Pick one or two depending on time. Ask the audience, then give the model answer.]

Card A. Mei Ling and Zul are offered a hotel job abroad. When they arrive, their passports are taken and they are told they owe a debt. What is this? It is trafficking, and section 372 and the Anti-Trafficking Act cover it. Hadith Bukhari 2227 is against the one who sells a free person. What can a friend do? Contact the embassy or the police and the family, and report.

Card B. After the exam, friends say, "Let us go to that place, everyone does it once." You feel the pressure. What is the staircase step? It is an invitation, the first step. What do you do? Say no clearly, leave, and tell a trusted friend. Remember the Prophet's questions, "Would you like it for your sister?" And remember the buyer is part of the market.

Card C. A woman returns to the village after leaving sex work. Neighbours whisper. What does Islam say? Al-Furqan 70, the door of tawbah is open. We should not spread her past, because Allah warned about spreading immorality in An-Nur 19. We help her find work and be part of the community again.

## Slide 54 | Conclusion: three messages (8:45 to 9:45)

To conclude, three messages for all of us as Muslims.

One. Guard the first step. Allah said do not even approach zina. If we close the early doors, the gaze, the private chat, the secret offer, we protect ourselves and others from a path that is hard to leave.

Two. Condemn the exploitation and protect the weak. Do not be a buyer, a middleman, or a silent bystander. Remember Surah An-Nur: Verse 33. The one who forces is accountable and the one who was forced is shown mercy.

Three. Keep the door of tawbah open for everyone. Do not let our community push a person back by shame. Allah replaces evil deeds with good for the one who repents.

## Slide 55 | Closing du'a and back to the poll (9:45 to 10:15)

Let us go back to our four statements. Selling sex is zina with a price, true. Only the seller is sinful, false, the buyer and the middleman are accountable. Everyone chose it freely, false in many cases, because of deception, debt and force. A person who leaves can be forgiven, true, and that is Allah's promise in Al-Furqan 70.

We will finish with the du'a of the Prophet (SAW) for the young man, which is a du'a for all of us. *Allahummaghfir dhanbah, wa tahhir qalbah, wa hassin farjah.* O Allah, forgive our sins, purify our hearts, and guard our chastity. Ameen.
