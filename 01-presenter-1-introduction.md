## Slide 4 | The woman and the dog (1:50 to 3:15)

Let me start with a story from Sahih al-Bukhari, number 3467. Abu Hurairah reported that the Prophet (SAW) said: a dog was circling a well and was about to die of thirst. A woman from Bani Israel, who was a prostitute, saw it. She took off her leather sock, lowered it into the well and gave the dog water. Allah forgave her because of that.

[Pause for two seconds.]

Think about that for a moment. The Prophet (SAW) did not hide what her life was. He called her what she was. And still, Allah looked at one sincere act of mercy and forgave her.

This story is not a licence. Nobody can say "so the act must be fine". The act is forbidden, and we will show the evidence for that clearly. What the story tells us is the attitude we want to keep today. Islam is firm about the sin and the exploitation around it, and Islam never closes the door of mercy on a human being.

## Slide 5 | Our approach: Protect, Prevent, Restore (3:15 to 4:00)

Because of that story, we built this presentation around three words. Protect the vulnerable from exploitation. Prevent people from taking the first step. Restore people who want to leave. You will see these three words again in our conclusion.

Two ground rules for the room. First, this is a serious topic, so please no jokes about people. Second, many people caught in prostitution are victims of deception, debt or force, and we will talk about them with respect.

## Slide 7 | What is prostitution? (5:00 to 6:15)

Let us define it from three angles.

In language, the Arabic word is bigha'. It comes from the root ba, ghayn, ya, which carries the meaning of seeking and also of transgressing. The woman is called a baghiy. Allah uses the word bigha' in Surah An-Nur, verse 33. In Malay we say pelacuran.

In fiqh, prostitution is zina done for a price. Zina means sexual relations outside a valid marriage. Adding money to it does not change the ruling on zina, and it adds a second problem, because the money itself is forbidden. Presenter 3 will show the hadith about that.

In Malaysian law, section 372(3) of the Penal Code says prostitution is "the act of a person offering that person's body for sexual gratification for hire whether in money or in kind". Please notice the last words, "in kind". Payment can be gifts, allowances, a phone or a holiday. It does not have to be cash.

## Slide 8 | Key terms (6:15 to 7:00)

Here are the terms you will hear from us today. Zina, unlawful sexual relations. Bigha' and baghiy, the act and the woman. Mahr al-baghiy, the fee earned from prostitution. Fahisha, an abomination, which is the word the Quran uses for zina. Khalwat, being alone together with someone of the opposite sex who is not your spouse or mahram. Muncikari, a procurer, a person who arranges it. Our Syariah law defines muncikari as a person who acts as a procurer between a female and a male for any purpose contrary to Islamic law. And tawbah, sincere repentance.

[If short on time, skip this slide. Explain each term when it first appears.]

## Slide 9 | Zina, prostitution and trafficking (7:00 to 7:50)

These three ideas overlap but they are not the same. Zina is the widest. Prostitution is zina with payment. Trafficking adds force, fraud or exploitation, and in trafficking the person is a victim of a crime.

This matters for the Islamic view. The ruling on the act is clear. But the blame is not the same for everyone. Someone who is forced is in a very different position from someone who profits. We will see in Surah An-Nur 33 that Allah promises forgiveness to women who were compelled.

## Slide 10 | Forms today (7:50 to 8:40)

Prostitution today is not only a street corner. It happens on the street, in massage or entertainment premises, through escort agencies, and increasingly online, through social media, Telegram groups and webcams. There are also so-called sugar arrangements, where allowances and gifts are exchanged for intimacy. If sex is traded for money or benefits, it is the same ruling, and the Malaysian definition covers "in kind" payment. And at the darkest end there is trafficking and forced prostitution.

[If short on time, skip this slide and say one sentence about online forms.]

## Slide 11 | The scale of the problem (8:40 to 9:40)

Now some numbers, and a warning first. This is a hidden activity, so every number is an estimate.

Globally, the International Labour Organization, Walk Free and IOM estimated in 2022 that 50 million people lived in modern slavery in 2021. Of all people in forced labour, 23 percent were in forced commercial sexual exploitation. Almost four in five of them were women or girls. And among children in forced labour, more than half were in commercial sexual exploitation.

In Malaysia, the Ministry of Health estimated about 37,000 women, cisgender and transgender, are sex workers. A study in Greater Kuala Lumpur found HIV prevalence of 11.7 percent among sex workers, compared with 0.4 percent among adults in general. Presenter 4 will return to this.

And the US State Department's 2024 report on Malaysia said that in the period it covered, 165 trafficking victims were formally identified, and 29 of them were victims of sex trafficking.

## Slide 12 | Why this matters to us (9:40 to 10:20)

Why are we, a group of students, talking about this? Because we are young, we are online most of the day, many of us are away from our families, and some of us feel money pressure. The first step toward this usually does not look like prostitution. It looks like a message from a stranger, an easy offer, or a friend's invitation. Islam teaches us to look at the first step, and that is exactly the idea Presenter 2 will start with.
