## Slide 14 | Why does it happen? (0:15 to 1:45)

There is rarely one reason. Researchers talk about push factors and pull factors, and both apply here.

Push factors are the pressures that drive a person out of their situation. Poverty and debt. A broken family or abuse at home. Having no support, for example being a runaway or an orphan. Lack of education and job skills. Being a migrant or a stateless person with no legal work. Drug or alcohol dependence in some cases. Weak faith and a weak sense of haya', which is shame before Allah.

Pull factors are the things that attract or trap a person. Promises of easy money. A job advertisement that turns out to be fake. A boyfriend or "friend" who grooms the person and later controls them. Peer pressure and a lifestyle that costs more than a person earns. And demand, because without buyers there is no market.

[Look at the audience.]

A point to remember. In the same US State Department report that Presenter 1 quoted, traffickers in Malaysia recruit young women with offers of legal work in restaurants, hotels and salons, or brokered marriages, and then exploit them in sex trafficking. So for many people the "choice" was never a free choice.

## Slide 15 | The staircase: footsteps of Shaytan (1:45 to 3:00)

Next, how does a person get there? Allah says in Surah An-Nur verse 21, "Do not follow the footsteps of Shaytan". The word is footsteps, which means steps, one at a time, and nobody jumps to the bottom in a single move.

On the slide you can see the staircase. It starts with the gaze. Then a message or a conversation. Then flirting and a meeting. Then khalwat, being alone together. Then the touching and the sin itself. And at the bottom, when money is involved, a person is selling or buying.

Islam puts its guards at the top of the staircase, not at the bottom. That is why the Quran says do not even approach zina, and why the Prophet (SAW) said, in Tirmidhi 2165, that when a man and a woman are alone, the third of them is Shaytan. Presenter 3 will show these.

## Slide 16 | Who is involved? (3:00 to 4:00)

Prostitution is not only about one person. At least four roles are involved, and Islam treats each of them.

The seller. This is the person who sells sex. The act is forbidden, and tawbah is open. Many in this role are under pressure or force, so blame is not equal.

The buyer. The one who pays is also committing zina, and we should be honest that he is half of the market. Many people talk only about the seller.

The middleman. The pimp, the agent, the person who advertises. Our Syariah law calls this person the muncikari. The Quran, in Surah Al-Ma'idah verse 2, says do not cooperate in sin and aggression.

The exploiter and trafficker. The person who forces, tricks or sells another person. Allah says in Surah An-Nur 33 do not compel your slave girls to prostitution. And in Bukhari 2227, Allah says He will be against the one who sells a free person and eats the price.

## Slide 17 | Malaysian law 1: Penal Code (4:00 to 5:00)

Now to the law. Malaysia has two layers, the federal criminal law, and Syariah law for Muslims.

In the federal Penal Code, section 372 punishes exploiting a person for prostitution. That includes selling or buying a person for that purpose, bringing a person in or out of the country by deceit, holding a person, advertising a prostitution service, or acting as the intermediary. The punishment can reach 15 years in prison, caning and a fine.

Section 372A punishes living on the earnings of another person's prostitution, up to 15 years, caning and a fine. And section 372B punishes soliciting or importuning for the purpose of prostitution, up to one year, or a fine, or both.

Also, the Anti-Trafficking in Persons and Anti-Smuggling of Migrants Act 2007 deals with sex trafficking, and the US report says the penalty is three to 20 years.

## Slide 18 | Malaysian law 2: Syariah offences (5:00 to 6:00)

For Muslims, the Syariah Criminal Offences (Federal Territories) Act 1997 has a block of offences against decency. Section 21 is prostitution. Section 22 is muncikari. Section 23 is sexual intercourse out of wedlock, for both a man and a woman. Section 24 is any act preparatory to sexual intercourse out of wedlock. And section 27 is khalwat.

The penalties are capped by the federal limit for Syariah courts, which is up to three years, RM5,000 and six strokes of the cane for the main offences, and up to two years and RM3,000 for khalwat and preparatory acts. Each state has its own enactment, and the Federal Territories one is our example.

An honest note. Critics point out that section 21 names "any woman who prostitutes herself", and the buyer is caught under the other sections, like section 23. We simply report the law, and we think the principle of Islam is that both sides are accountable.

## Slide 19 | How the world handles it (6:00 to 7:00)

Quickly, four models around the world.

One, criminalise everything, sellers and buyers. The risk is that victims get punished.
Two, legalise and regulate, like the Netherlands and Germany. The idea is to control and protect, but we will see Presenter 4's evidence that this may increase trafficking.
Three, the Nordic model, criminalise the buyer and the pimp but not the seller, which Sweden started in 1999. It targets demand.
Four, full decriminalisation, like New Zealand's 2003 law.

And Islam's approach, which sits differently. It forbids the act. It also fights the exploitation. It protects those who were compelled. It sets a very high bar of proof before any punishment. And most of its weight goes to prevention and to opening a lawful alternative, which is marriage, and to opening the door of tawbah. [Briefly.] We are not saying one government model is the Islamic one. We are saying Islam has its own logic.

## Slide 20 | The maqasid lens (7:00 to 8:00)

Scholars summarise the aims of the Shariah in five things to protect: religion, life, intellect, lineage and wealth. Let us see how prostitution damages each one.

Religion. It breaks Allah's limits and normalises fahisha. Allah warns about those who love that immorality should spread, in Surah An-Nur verse 19.
Life. Violence, disease and trauma, and Presenter 4 has the numbers.
Intellect. Deception, grooming and addiction used as chains.
Lineage. Unclear fatherhood, unwanted pregnancies and children who grow up without a family.
Wealth. The income is haram, and organised crime and debt bondage profit from it.

So when Islam prohibits it, it is protecting five things at once.

## Slide 21 | Case 1: Aina (8:00 to 9:00)

[Read the case slowly.]

Aina is 20 years old. She is a university student with RM3,000 of debt from tuition and phone instalments. A stranger on an app offers her RM2,000 for "just a dinner". Please discuss with the person next to you for 30 seconds. What are the red flags? What step is she standing on, on our staircase? What can she do instead?

[Take two or three answers. Then give the model answer below.]

Model answer. Red flags: a stranger, a large payment for a small thing, secrecy, and an app. The staircase: she is at the step of "message and offer" and the next step is a meeting alone. What she can do: block and report, talk to a trusted person or a university counsellor, ask the university about financial aid and zakat funds, and look for lawful part-time work. And an important point for us as friends. We should be the person she can tell without being judged.
