## Slide 9 | Part 2 divider (0:00 to 0:10)

Thank you, Presenter 1. In this part we ask three questions. Why does it happen, how does a person end up there, and how do the law and Islam respond.

## Slide 10 | Why does it happen? (0:10 to 1:15)

There is rarely one reason. Researchers speak of push factors and pull factors, and both apply.

Push factors are the pressures that drive a person out of their situation. Poverty and debt. A broken family or abuse at home. No support, for example a runaway, an orphan or a stateless person. Little education or few job skills. Weak faith and a weak sense of haya', which is shame before Allah.

Pull factors attract or trap a person. Promises of easy money. A fake job advertisement. A boyfriend or "friend" who grooms a person and later controls them. Peer pressure and a lifestyle that costs more than the person earns. And demand, because without buyers there is no market.

In the US State Department report that Presenter 1 quoted, traffickers in Malaysia recruit young women with offers of legal work in restaurants, hotels and salons, then exploit them. For many people the "choice" was never free.

## Slide 11 | The staircase: footsteps of Shaytan (1:15 to 2:10)

How does a person get there? Allah says in Surah An-Nur: Verse 21, "Do not follow the footsteps of Shaytan". Footsteps means one step at a time. Nobody jumps to the bottom in one move.

On the slide, the staircase starts with the gaze. Then messages and talk. Then flirting and meeting. Then khalwat, being alone together. Then the sin itself, and at the bottom, selling or buying.

Islam puts its guards at the top of the staircase. That is why the Quran says do not even approach zina, and why the Prophet (SAW) said in Tirmidhi 2165 that when a man and a woman are alone, the third of them is Shaytan. Presenter 3 will show both.

## Slide 12 | Who is involved? (2:10 to 3:05)

At least four roles are involved, and Islam treats each one.

The seller. The act is forbidden and tawbah is open. Many are pressured or forced, so blame is not equal (Surah An-Nur: Verse 33).

The buyer. He also commits zina, and he is half of the market. Many people talk only about the seller (Surah An-Nur: Verse 2).

The middleman, the pimp, the agent, the advertiser. Our Syariah law calls this person the muncikari. The Quran says do not cooperate in sin and aggression (Surah Al-Ma'idah: Verse 2).

The exploiter or trafficker, who forces, tricks or sells another person. In Bukhari 2227, Allah says He will be against the one who sells a free person and eats the price.

## Slide 13 | Malaysian law 1: Penal Code (3:05 to 3:55)

Malaysia has two layers, the federal criminal law and Syariah law for Muslims. In the Penal Code, section 372 punishes exploiting a person for prostitution. That covers selling or buying a person, bringing a person in or out of the country by deceit, wrongful restraint, advertising, or acting as the intermediary. The penalty reaches 15 years, caning and a fine.

Section 372A punishes living on another person's earnings from prostitution, also up to 15 years, caning and a fine. Section 372B punishes soliciting, up to one year, or a fine, or both. And the Anti-Trafficking in Persons Act 2007 covers sex trafficking, with a penalty of 3 to 20 years according to the US report.

## Slide 14 | Malaysian law 2: Syariah offences (3:55 to 5:00)

For Muslims, the Syariah Criminal Offences (Federal Territories) Act 1997 has its own offences. Section 21 is prostitution. Section 22 is muncikari. Section 23 is sexual intercourse out of wedlock, for both a man and a woman. Section 24 is any act preparatory to it, and section 27 is khalwat.

Penalties are capped by the federal limit for Syariah courts, which is up to three years, RM5,000 and six strokes for the main offences, and up to two years and RM3,000 for khalwat and preparatory acts. Each state has its own enactment, and the Federal Territories one is our example.

Notice that sections 24 and 27 punish the steps before the act. The law prevents too, just like the staircase. One honest note. Section 21 names a woman who prostitutes herself, so the buyer is caught under other sections like section 23. We report the law as it is, and we say the Islamic principle holds both sides accountable.

## Backup slides for this part (hidden, not part of the live talk)

### Backup slide 48 | How the world handles it

Four models. One, criminalise everything, sellers and buyers. The risk is that victims get punished. Two, legalise and regulate, like the Netherlands and Germany. The idea is control and protection, but Presenter 4's evidence suggests it may enlarge the market and trafficking. Three, the Nordic model, which punishes buyers and pimps and not sellers, started by Sweden in 1999. It targets demand but is hard to enforce online. Four, full decriminalisation, like New Zealand in 2003, which treats it as ordinary work but does not remove coercion.

Islam's approach sits differently. It forbids the act, fights exploitation, protects those who were compelled, sets a very high bar of proof before any punishment, closes the early doors, and opens marriage and tawbah. It needs a caring community and real support. We are not saying one government model is the Islamic one.

### Backup slide 49 | The maqasid lens

Scholars summarise the aims of the Shariah in five things to protect. Religion, because it breaks Allah's limits and normalises fahisha (Surah An-Nur: Verse 19). Life, through violence, disease and trauma. Intellect, through deceit, grooming and addiction used as chains. Lineage, through unclear fatherhood and children without a family. Wealth, because the income is haram and organised crime and debt bondage profit from it. When Islam prohibits it, it protects five things at once.

### Backup slide 50 | Case 1: Aina

Aina is 20, a university student with RM3,000 of debt from tuition and phone instalments. A stranger on an app offers her RM2,000 for "just a dinner". Ask the audience to discuss for 30 seconds. What are the red flags? Which staircase step is she on? What can she do instead?

Model answer. Red flags: a stranger, a large payment for a small thing, secrecy, an app. She is at the step of "message and offer" and the next step is a meeting alone. She can block and report, talk to a trusted person or a university counsellor, ask about financial aid and zakat funds, and look for lawful part-time work. As friends, we should be the person she can tell without being judged.
